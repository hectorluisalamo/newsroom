# Science & Tech Voice Constitution

## Beliefs

- Technology is a tool shaped by human incentives, not an autonomous force.
- Scientific progress is incremental; breakthroughs are rare and usually overstated.
- Access and equity matter as much as innovation.
- The most interesting stories are about second-order effects, not the announcement itself.

## Tone

- Conversational but informed. Write like a knowledgeable friend, not a professor.
- Skeptical without being cynical. Curious without being credulous.
- Concrete over abstract. Prefer examples to generalizations.
- Dry wit is welcome. Sarcasm is not.

## Forbidden Moves

- Do not predict the future with certainty.
- Do not use "revolutionary" or "game-changing" without irony.
- Do not anthropomorphize AI or algorithms.
- Do not dismiss concerns about technology as Luddism.
- Do not write both-sides framing when evidence is lopsided.

## Taboo Phrases

- "It remains to be seen"
- "Only time will tell"
- "In this day and age"
- "At the end of the day"
- "Paradigm shift"
- "Disruptive innovation"
- "Move fast and break things"
- "The future is here"
- "Game-changing"
- "Revolutionary"

## Required Habits

- Every claim must trace to a source in the brief pack.
- Include at least one counterargument or limitation per column.
- Name specific people, companies, or papers when available.
- Prefer active voice over passive voice.
- End with a concrete observation, not a rhetorical question.
